
# RetailIQ — Project Decision Context

## 1. Project Overview

RetailIQ is a retail analytics and decision intelligence platform. It combines SQL analytics, statistical experimentation, causal machine learning, predictive modeling, forecasting, business optimization, and a local AI assistant.

The project uses two separate datasets. Their customer records must not be joined because they do not share customer identifiers.

## 2. Datasets

### Hillstrom Email Marketing Dataset

- Approximately 64,000 randomized campaign records.
- Groups: Men's E-Mail, Women's E-Mail, and No E-Mail.
- Outcomes include visits, conversions, and customer spend.
- Contains no customer ID.
- Used for campaign experimentation, statistical testing, uplift modeling, and campaign policy evaluation.

### UCI Online Retail II

- Approximately 1,067,371 transaction records.
- Covers December 2009 to December 2011.
- Used for SQL analytics, customer segmentation, propensity modeling, inactivity prediction, and revenue forecasting.
- Contains 5,863 identified customers in the RFM analysis.

| Group | Mean Spend |
|---|---:|
| Men's E-Mail | £1.4226 |
| No E-Mail | £0.6528 |
| Women's E-Mail | £1.0772 |

All three pairwise conversion comparisons were statistically significant after Holm correction.

Revenue comparisons using Welch tests and bootstrap confidence intervals were also significant after Holm correction.

Sample ratio mismatch test, assuming equal 1:1:1 allocation:
- Chi-square: 0.2025
- P-value: 0.9037
- No evidence of sample ratio mismatch.

These are historical experimental findings, not guaranteed future campaign outcomes.

## 4. Experiment Design

For a two-sided conversion-rate experiment:
- Required sample size: 26,233 per group.
- Total required sample size: 52,466.
- Approximate detectable effect with 21,306 per group: 0.2239 percentage points.

The randomization utility is intended for prospective experiments with unique subject IDs. Hillstrom does not contain customer IDs, so it cannot be retrospectively randomized at the customer level.

## 6. Campaign Policy Evaluation

Cross-fitted evaluation using five folds:

| Strategy | Estimated Conversion Rate | Incremental Conversions per 1,000 |
|---|---:|---:|
| No campaign | 0.573% | 0.00 |
| Random 30% allocation | 0.777% | 2.04 |
| Uplift-based 30% allocation | 0.798% | 2.25 |

The confidence intervals overlap. There is no conclusive evidence that uplift-based allocation outperforms random allocation.

## 7. Profit-Based Campaign Optimization

The campaign policy was evaluated using five-fold outer cross-fitting. Each customer's uplift prediction and targeting decision were generated by models trained without that customer's outer fold.

Illustrative email cost assumption: £0.01 per email.

| Strategy | Contact Rate | Revenue per Customer | Cost per Customer | Net Profit per Customer | Incremental Profit per 1,000 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| No campaign | 0.00% | £0.6528 | £0.0000 | £0.6528 | £0.00 |
| Random 30% allocation | 30.00% | £0.8837 | £0.0030 | £0.8807 | £227.95 |
| Uplift-based 30% allocation | 29.99% | £0.8237 | £0.0030 | £0.8207 | £167.94 |

Bootstrap confidence intervals for incremental profit per 1,000 customers:

- Random 30% allocation: £142.23 to £313.86.
- Uplift-based 30% allocation: -£1.53 to £337.45.

### Finding

Random 30% allocation produced the higher estimated incremental profit.

The uplift-based policy's confidence interval includes zero. The results do not establish that uplift targeting is profitable or superior to random allocation.

### Methodology and Limitations

- Five-fold outer cross-fitting was used to generate out-of-sample uplift predictions and targeting decisions.
- Policy value was estimated using inverse-propensity weighting.
- Bootstrap intervals use 2,000 customer-level resamples.
- Bootstrap intervals hold the learned outer-fold policy decisions fixed and do not include model-retraining uncertainty.
- Email cost is illustrative, not verified.
- Results estimate policy value in the historical randomized experiment and do not guarantee future campaign profit.

## 8. Predictive Modeling

### Purchase Propensity

Predicts purchase within 30 days using historical transaction features.

Chronological evaluation:

| Model | ROC-AUC | PR-AUC | Precision | Recall | F1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Logistic Regression | 0.7202 | 0.6599 | 0.6269 | 0.5221 | 0.5697 |
| Random Forest | 0.7152 | 0.6512 | 0.6103 | 0.5631 | 0.5857 |

Training customers: 2,784.
Testing customers: 3,240.

The chronological snapshots overlap in historical periods and include customer overlap. Results are preliminary and are not an independent holdout evaluation.


### Customer Inactivity Prediction

Predicts 90-day inactivity from 180 days of historical data.

Training cutoff: 2011-06-12.
Testing cutoff: 2011-09-10.

| Model | ROC-AUC | PR-AUC | Precision | Recall | F1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Logistic Regression | 0.7231 | 0.5664 | 0.5126 | 0.7836 | 0.6198 |
| Random Forest | 0.7279 | 0.5680 | 0.5239 | 0.7413 | 0.6139 |
| XGBoost | 0.7161 | 0.5513 | 0.5279 | 0.7554 | 0.6215 |

Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and XGBoost were evaluated.

- Random Forest achieved the highest ROC-AUC and PR-AUC.
- XGBoost achieved the highest F1 and precision.
- Logistic Regression achieved the highest recall.

The preferred model depends on the evaluation metric and business objective. No model performed best across all reported metrics.

These results are preliminary and use one historical training cutoff and one later testing cutoff. They do not establish generalization across multiple historical periods.


## 9. Revenue Forecasting

Monthly merchandise revenue was forecast using:

- Seasonal Naive baseline.
- Holt-Winters additive trend model without a seasonal component.

| Model | MAE | RMSE | MAPE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Seasonal Naive | £147,508.75 | £255,044.70 | 21.53% |
| Holt-Winters | £274,734.11 | £395,445.27 | 23.10% |

The Seasonal Naive model performed better on all three reported metrics.

The Holt-Winters model did not improve forecast accuracy. The available training history was insufficient for a reliable full seasonal model.
